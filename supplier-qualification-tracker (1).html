Supplier Qualification Tracker

A lightweight version of PPAP, the Production Part Approval Process manufacturers use to formally prove a supplier's part is ready. Six stages, one visible trail, and an automatic flag when something already approved quietly breaks again.

Built for anyone sourcing physical products long-term, whether that's motorcycle parts, leather bags, or toys: fast enough to keep a startup moving, thorough enough to catch what actually matters once real customers get their hands on it.

A free tool by Sylvia Wanjiru Opiyo, Operational Excellence & Quality Leader.

The process: agree the spec, get a sample, validate it, prove it holds across several batches, close what's open, sign off. The part most trackers miss: regressions, where something already fixed quietly breaks again.

Why regressions matter more than first failures

A first-time failure is normal, that's what the process is for. A supplier failing something it already fixed usually means there's no real process control, just manual firefighting of whatever was flagged last. That pattern predicts scale problems far better than any single pass rate, which is why the Batch Log flags it automatically.

The six stages, explained

01

Spec & design agreement

Document the target spec and get explicit agreement from the supplier.

02

Initial sample submitted

First article received against that agreed spec.

03

Sample validated

Fitment, function, and performance checked against every spec, not just the obvious ones.

04

Process consistency proven

Several batches run and hold steady, not just one lucky sample.

05

Open issues resolved

Everything flagged along the way is closed out, with evidence.

06

Final approval / sign-off

Formally locked in as a standing partner, not just an approved sample.
