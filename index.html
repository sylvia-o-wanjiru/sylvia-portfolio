Supplier Development · GOGO Electric

From 60% scrap to zero: building a CKD supplier partnership from scratch

No qualified supplier existed for the CKD parts we needed: rims, frames, wiring harnesses. Up to 60% of high-value components were being scrapped, 30% of every cargo needed rework, and there was no proven product-market fit for the spec.
0%
scrap rate after 4 months
Benchmark

Finding the target spec

Identified a proven, high-quality reference product already trusted in the Uganda market and used it to document the exact specification we needed suppliers to hit.

Trial

Testing three suppliers in parallel

Sent the documented spec and physical samples to three Chinese manufacturers, then had each produce their own sample batch to match it.

Validate

Fitment, function, spec

Every sample went through fitment, functionality, and specification checks. Gaps were addressed on joint calls with the supplier until each one demonstrably closed them.

Iterate

Batch-by-batch feedback loop

Each production batch was reviewed, feedback given, and the "good" standard locked in a little tighter. This turned one-off inspection into a repeatable improvement cycle.

Two reliable suppliers, by batch six

By the sixth batch, two suppliers had shown consistent spec adherence and fast iteration. Those became the standing CKD partners.

Quality Systems & Compliance · GOGO Electric

Building a compliance backbone the team actually owns

Compliance was reactive and inconsistent, and operational knowledge lived in scattered documents with no version control. The goal wasn't just to fix the gaps. It was to build a system the people running it would actually keep using.
83%
compliance, up from 46%
Foundation

One system of record

Built a Google Workspace knowledge management system structuring 500+ controlled documents (from policies to work instructions) with automated numbering, version control, and review tracking.

Expand

Reusing the framework, not duplicating it

When the company needed an environmental & health management system, it was layered onto the existing QMS: same risk tracker, same audit schedule, an expanded management review scope, instead of building a parallel structure.

Audit rhythm

A predictable, scalable cycle

Introduced a layered audit process and quarterly planning cycle so compliance activity stayed tied to business goals instead of being a once-a-year scramble.

Ownership

Process owners write the policy

Process owners and top management developed and owned their own documentation, policies, and OKRs from day one, building the habit of maintaining the system, not just the system itself.
