The framework: Cost of Quality (COQ) is split into four categories: Prevention (planning, training, supplier development), Appraisal (inspection, testing, QC staff), Internal Failure (scrap, rework caught before shipping), and External Failure (warranty claims, returns, recalls). Internal and External Failure together are the "Cost of Poor Quality." That's what you're actually losing, as opposed to what you're investing to prevent losses.

The four categories, explained

Prevention

Money spent before anything goes wrong: training, quality planning, supplier development, preventive maintenance.

Appraisal

The cost of checking: QC staff salaries, inspection, testing, audits.

Internal failure

Defects caught before shipping: scrap, rework, downtime, re-inspection.

External failure

Defects the customer finds: warranty claims, returns, recalls, lost trust.

This is a directional estimate, not an audit. Real Cost of Quality work usually needs finance and operations data pulled together over a full quarter to be precise. This tool exists to make the scale of the problem visible, not to replace that process.
